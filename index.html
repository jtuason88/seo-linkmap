What the crawl found

Header and footer navigation links were excluded from every count below. They appear identically on roughly a hundred pages and carry little topical signal. What's measured here is contextual links inside body copy.

Problem 1

BoothOS is a link sink

It receives “Explore BoothOS” from the Talent Solutions and Managed Solutions pages and sends nothing back out — zero body links of any kind. Authority flows in and stops. It also has no H1 in the markup at all.

Problem 2

Industries and Talent Hubs are invisible

The footer links all six Solutions from every page on the site, and links zero Industries and zero Talent Hubs. Seven commercial pages get no sitewide link equity, and none of them receive a single contextual link either.

Problem 3

Three blog posts absorb everything

The same three featured posts appear on nine of ten staffing role pages and both location pages. Meanwhile 70+ published posts receive no internal links, including eight Philippines posts and nine Colombia posts that map perfectly to pages that link none of them.

How to work it

Open the Link map tab, filter to P1, and sort by source page. That groups the work into one pass per URL. The Excel tracker has the same rows with Status and Owner columns for tracking. Start with the four Solutions hubs — they carry the most authority and currently leak the most.

Where the anchors sit

No recommendation puts a link inside header-tagged text. Heading copy is usually template-controlled, and an anchor inside a heading competes with that heading's own relevance signal. Where a link would naturally have landed on a heading, it's been re-anchored into body copy, a list item, or a short new sentence.

The one case that needed real thought. On all five industry pages, the job titles under “X Experts, Ready to Deliver” are H3 headings with no body copy beneath them — there's simply no prose to attach a link to. Rather than link the headings, the map specifies one new sentence beneath each role card grid, with the exact wording drafted per page in the Link map tab.
One caution on anchor text. Apply the anchors as written. They're deliberately varied so no commercial phrase repeats verbatim across pages. Where an anchor does repeat it's a proper noun — BoothOS, BoothAgent, Colombia, the Philippines — which is natural branded anchor text. Bulk-applying a single exact-match phrase sitewide is the failure mode that turns an internal linking project into an over-optimisation problem.
